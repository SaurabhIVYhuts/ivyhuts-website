Moving abroad for higher studies is an exciting experience, but finding the right student accommodation abroad can be one of the biggest decisions you make as an international student.

Your accommodation is more than just a place to sleep. It can affect your daily routine, travel time, study experience, budget, safety, and overall student life.

With so many accommodation options available, it can be difficult to know what to look for. This student accommodation guide explains what to consider when choosing accommodation for international students, from location and budget to safety, contracts and lifestyle.

## 1. Choose a Convenient Location Near Your University

Location should be one of the first things you check when looking for student accommodation.

Ideally, your accommodation should have convenient access to:

* Your university or college
* Public transport
* Grocery stores and supermarkets
* Hospitals and pharmacies
* Restaurants and essential services
* Libraries and study spaces

A cheaper room that is very far from your university may eventually cost you more in transportation and time.

Before booking, check the distance between the accommodation and your campus and understand how you will travel every day. You can use [University Housing](/university-housing) on IvyHuts to explore student accommodation near your university.

## 2. Consider Your Budget and the Total Cost

Budget is an important part of choosing accommodation abroad.

Don't look only at the monthly rent. You should also check whether additional costs are included, such as:

* Electricity
* Water
* Heating
* Internet
* Maintenance charges
* Laundry
* Security deposit
* Transportation

Make a complete monthly budget before making your decision.

Choosing affordable student accommodation that fits comfortably within your budget can help you manage your finances better throughout your studies. To estimate your wider living costs, try the IvyHuts [Student Planner](/student-planner).

## 3. Check Safety and Security

Safety should never be compromised when choosing a place to live, especially when you are moving to a new country.

Before booking accommodation, check the available security features, such as:

* Secure building access
* CCTV or security systems
* Well-maintained common areas
* Emergency support
* Safe surroundings
* Proper lighting around the property

You can also research the neighbourhood and read reviews from other students to understand their experiences. Safe student accommodation should feel secure both inside the building and in the surrounding area.

## 4. Understand the Types of Student Accommodation

Student accommodation can come in different formats, depending on the property and location.

You may find options such as:

* Private rooms
* Shared rooms
* Studio apartments
* Ensuite rooms
* Shared apartments

Think about your lifestyle and preferences before choosing.

If you prefer privacy and a quiet environment, a private room or studio may be suitable. If you want to meet other students and reduce costs, shared accommodation can be an option.

## 5. Check the Available Amenities

Amenities can make your everyday student life much easier.

Depending on the accommodation, useful facilities may include:

* Wi-Fi
* Laundry facilities
* Kitchen or cooking facilities
* Study areas
* Common rooms
* Fitness facilities
* Storage space

However, don't choose accommodation simply because it has many facilities. Focus on the amenities you will actually use.

## 6. Think About Your Study Environment

As a student, your accommodation should support your academic routine.

Ask yourself:

* Is the room quiet enough for studying?
* Is there a proper desk or study area?
* Is the internet reliable?
* Are common areas comfortable?
* Will you be able to maintain a regular study schedule?

A comfortable study environment can make a significant difference, especially during exams and assignment deadlines.

## 7. Read the Accommodation Contract Carefully

Before signing an accommodation agreement, read all the terms carefully.

Pay attention to:

* Rental period
* Payment schedule
* Security deposit
* Cancellation policy
* Notice period
* House rules
* Guest policies
* Maintenance responsibilities

If there is anything you don't understand, ask the accommodation provider for clarification before signing.

Never rush into a booking simply because you are worried that the room may become unavailable.

## 8. Check Reviews and Property Information

Online photos can give you an idea of what an accommodation looks like, but they may not tell you everything.

Look for genuine student reviews and check information about:

* Cleanliness
* Location
* Noise levels
* Maintenance
* Internet quality
* Management response
* Overall student experience

Comparing information from multiple sources can help you make a more informed decision.

## 9. Think About Your Lifestyle

The best student accommodation is not necessarily the most expensive one. It is the one that fits your lifestyle and needs.

For example, you may prefer:

* A quiet place for studying
* A social environment with other students
* Private accommodation
* Shared living
* Accommodation close to public transport
* A location near shops and essential services

Understanding your priorities will make it easier to compare different options.

## 10. Compare Your Options Before You Book

Don't choose the first accommodation you find.

Create a simple comparison of the key things to check before booking student accommodation:

| Factor    | What to Check                            |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Location  | Distance from university and transport   |
| Cost      | Rent + additional expenses               |
| Safety    | Security and surrounding area            |
| Room      | Size, privacy and facilities             |
| Amenities | Wi-Fi, laundry, study spaces, etc.       |
| Contract  | Duration, deposit and cancellation terms |
| Reviews   | Experiences of previous students         |

This makes it easier to identify which option provides the best overall value for you.
